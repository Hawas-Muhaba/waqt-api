How it works

How Waqt computes time across continuous solar horizons.

A location and an instant go in. A day boundary, a reading, the day’s events and a Hijri date come out — each computed once, by the API, and returned in one envelope.

Model

The continuous timeline

Formal definition

DAY
Day(L, D) = [ Maghrib(L, D) , Maghrib(L, D+1) ) — half-open, so every instant belongs to exactly one day.
READING
reading(t) = t − Maghrib(L, D), in SI seconds. Hours are 60 minutes; the reading resets at the next Maghrib.
LENGTH
The day’s length is measured from the two boundaries. It is not assumed to be 24 hours and can differ by place and season.
EVENTS
Fajr, sunrise, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha are instants, each also given as a reading on the day. An event after the closing Maghrib reads past the day length (Isha usually does).

Engine

Astronomy and honest accuracy

The production engine is a NOAA-style low-precision solar model, accuracy level B. Level A (sub-10-second against a high-precision ephemeris) is not claimed.

Independent verification

The project verifies its engine against separate references — a VSOP87-based solar model and an independent JPL DE421 + Skyfield reference. In the project’s own measurements the engine agrees to about a second at low and mid latitudes; in a five-date high-latitude check the error reached 9.3 seconds (see docs/ASTRONOMY.md).

That is good for a wall display, a timetable or a scheduler; it is not yet verified for legal or liturgical use without independent confirmation.

Edge cases

Determinacy at extreme latitudes

When the sun does not set

Near the poles around the solstices Maghrib does not occur. Waqt does not invent a time: the response is status: "UNDEFINED" with the reason, and no high-latitude fallback is applied automatically. Choosing a fallback is a human, jurisdictional decision.

Pipeline

The end-to-end pipeline

  1. 01

    Requestlat, lon and optionally an instant and conventions.

  2. 02

    Place & timezoneTimezone and local civil date are resolved.

  3. 03

    AstronomySolar position in Terrestrial Time gives sunset, sunrise, transit and the angle events.

  4. 04

    Day & eventsMaghrib-to-Maghrib day, reading, events and the next event.

  5. 05

    Calendar & envelopeA named Hijri provider adds the date; everything returns in {meta, data, error}.

Method

Convention invariance, live

Change the Fajr angle and ask the API again. Fajr moves; the Maghrib boundaries do not — because conventions govern only their own event.
