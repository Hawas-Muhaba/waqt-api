## Context
Both implementations evaluated the Sun's declination at the upper transit to obtain the noon shadow, but nothing said so, and Sprint 15 measured that the alternative convention would move Asr by up to 5.9 s, more than the 5 s band. Separately, at high latitude in winter the noon shadow is undefined (Sun at or below the horizon at noon) and the first independent run produced a nonsensical Asr there.

## Decision
AST-001: the target altitude uses the declination at upper transit (the literal 'noon shadow'); the crossing is solved with the Sun's position at the crossing. AST-002: Asr is undefined exactly when the geometric altitude at upper transit is <= 0 degrees.

## Evidence
137 day-model vectors carry an Asr event under the 5 s band and now name AST-001. The reference's code already implemented both rules (`solar.py`); no code changed and the engine revision stays `2`. The 5.9 s figure is Sprint 15's measurement, not re-measured.

## Consequences
Text and tags only. The Asr accuracy band stays at 5 s.

## Alternatives considered
Evaluate declination at the Asr instant and widen the band to 8 s: rejected, it changes the definition rather than refining it, and loosens the contract for every implementation.

## Configurability (religious / regional conventions)
The *shadow ratio* is a jurisprudential choice (commonly 1, and 2 in the Hanafi school) and remains a free parameter; neither value is ruled correct. Fajr and Isha angles likewise remain parameters. Only the astronomical convention is fixed here.

## Dissent and unresolved points
None.

## Reopen when
A published authority specifying a different noon-shadow convention; or a high-precision engine revision that changes the declination model.

## Vectors affected
day-model: 137 vectors tagged AST-001; asr/degenerate-67N-solstice promoted (AST-002).
